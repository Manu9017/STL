FRP pultruded profiles and resins

Glass-fibre reinforced plastic (FRP) sections made on our own pultrusion line in Ghaziabad. They do not rust, do not conduct electricity and weigh about a quarter as much as steel. We also supply isophthalic polyester and vinyl ester resins.

How we make them

Pultrusion pulls glass fibre through resin and a heated die. The profile comes out cured, with the same section along its whole length.

  1. Creel

    Packages of glass-fibre roving sit on a rack and feed continuous strands into the line. Continuous strand mat and a surface veil are added for strength across the profile and a smooth, resin-rich face.

  2. Resin bath

    The glass is wetted right through with resin mixed with filler, catalyst, pigment and, where needed, UV inhibitor or fire retardant. Dry glass inside a profile is a weak spot, so wet-out is checked all the time.

  3. Preformer

    Guide plates gather the wet glass into the rough shape of the section and squeeze out excess resin before the die.

  4. Heated die

    A chrome-plated steel die, heated in zones, gives the exact shape. The resin cures as the material travels through, so the profile leaves the die solid.

  5. Pullers

    Two clamps take turns gripping the cured profile and pulling, so the line runs without stopping. Pull speed and die temperature are set together for each section.

  6. Cut-off saw

    A diamond saw travels with the profile and cuts it to length. Lengths are cut to your order.

Where they are used

Acid shops and pickling

Gratings, walkways, platforms and handrails over pickling, flux and plating tanks, where steel corrodes in months.

Galvanizing plants

Structures and walkways in enclosed acid rooms, alongside SHIVAS Technology pretreatment lines.

Fume scrubbers and effluent plants

Structures, stairs and handrails where chemicals, salt or chlorine are in the air.

Coastal and marine

Jetty walkways, railings and fencing in salt air.

Telecom and electronics

Non-magnetic and radio-transparent supports and enclosures.

Design notes for engineers

  • Design for deflection, not just strength. FRP is about a ninth as stiff as steel, so spans are usually set by how much they bend, not by when they break.
  • Allow for long-term load. Under a load that stays on for years, FRP slowly creeps; use reduced design values for permanent loads.
  • Connect with bolts and bonding. Use stainless steel or FRP bolts; drill holes, do not punch them. Bonded joints add stiffness.
  • Cut and drill with ordinary tools fitted with carbide or diamond blades. Wear a dust mask, and seal cut ends with resin.
  • Check temperature. Standard resins suit normal ambient and warm process areas; tell us the temperature and we select the resin.
  • Choose the resin for the chemicals. Vinyl ester for strong acids, alkalis and chlorine; polyester for general use.

Questions

Do the profiles need painting?

No. The colour runs right through the profile and the surface veil protects the face.

Can my fitters cut and drill them on site?

Yes, with ordinary power tools fitted with carbide or diamond blades. Seal cut ends with resin.

Is FRP as strong as steel?

Weight for weight it is strong, but it is much less stiff. We size sections so they do not bend too much under your load.

Will it conduct electricity?

No. FRP is an insulator, which is why it is used for cable trays, ladders and rails near live equipment.

Can you make a section that is not in your list?

Send the drawing. A new section needs its own die; we tell you what that involves before you order.
