Why spray roasting

Spray roasting is the only route that recovers both the free acid and the acid bound as ferrous chloride, delivers a fine, saleable oxide and has the deepest reference base for strip and wire pickling lines at this size. Fluidised-bed roasting reaches the same chloride recovery but produces granular oxide with a lower market value and needs tighter bed control. Retardation units recover only the free acid (about a fifth of the chloride) and leave an iron-rich stream for disposal. Low-temperature evaporation suits small plants and ferrous-chloride sales but not full regeneration.

Scores are 1 (poor) to 5 (best); weights reflect a continuous 10 m³/h duty where acid purchase and SPL disposal dominate the economics.

Process description

How ARP-10 turns spent liquor into fresh acid and iron oxide.

1 Storage and feed (Area 100)

Spent liquor from the pickling line enters two 200 m³ FRP tanks T-101A/B (40 h buffer) that settle sludge and skim oil. Feed pumps P-101A/B deliver 10 m³/h through self-cleaning 50 µm filters F-101A/B, a magnetic flowmeter and a Coriolis densitometer to the venturi separator. Feed flow follows the separator level (LIC-2001). Off-spec liquor and filter back-flush go to drain tank T-102.

2 Venturi pre-concentration (Area 200)

Roaster off-gas leaves the cyclones at about 390 °C and enters venturi V-201, where 379 m³/h of liquor is injected at the throat (≈ 32 m/s). The hot gas evaporates 2.68 t/h of water and is cooled to 95 °C; the liquor concentrates from Fe 127 to 171 g/L. Separator S-201 disengages the droplets. Roaster feed pumps P-202A/B take 7.45 m³/h of pre-concentrate through duplex strainers to the lances at 6 bar. Pre-concentrate density is held by trimming the roaster top temperature; high density opens dilution water. An elevated 30 m³ quench tank T-202 floods the venturi by gravity through fail-open valve XV-2020 on high gas temperature or loss of circulation.

3 Spray roasting (Area 300)

Roaster R-301 is a 6.4 m internal-diameter, 496 m³ shell lined with 230 mm acid-resistant brick and 115 mm insulating brick. Six of eight roof lances atomise the pre-concentrate into hollow cones. The droplets dry and the iron chlorides hydrolyse as they fall through the hot gas:

2 FeCl₂ + 2 H₂O + ½ O₂ → Fe₂O₃ + 4 HCl    2 FeCl₃ + 3 H₂O → Fe₂O₃ + 6 HCl

Four 2.75 MW tangential burners at +11.800 fire 808 SCM/h of natural gas (7.88 MW) with 9,663 Nm³/h of combustion air, holding 650–700 °C in the burner zone and 400 °C at the roof. Gas residence time is about 38 s at 0.41 m/s superficial velocity. Oxygen is trimmed to 3 % (dry) at the outlet. Each burner follows the SHIVAS combustion arrangement: common main gas train with double block-and-vent, per-burner double SSOVs, air/gas ratio control, UV flame scanner and burner control unit under a SIL 2 burner management system.

4 Oxide handling (Area 700)

Iron oxide (1,816 kg/h) collects in the cone and leaves through rotary valve RV-301 and lump breaker LB-301. A lean-phase pneumatic system (Roots blowers B-701A/B) conveys it 85 m to silo SI-701 (270 m³, 2.8 days) with a pulse-jet bag filter. Dispatch is by 1 t big-bags (BB-701) or bulk tanker (TL-701). Design quality: Fe₂O₃ 98.7 %, Cl 0.12 %.

5 Gas path (Areas 300–500)

Roaster gas (19,145 Nm³/h) passes cyclones C-301A/B, which return fines to the roaster through aerated chutes, then the venturi. The saturated gas (22,483 Nm³/h at 95 °C; N₂ 32.9, O₂ 1.1, CO₂ 3.6, H₂O 56.7, HCl 5.7 vol%) enters adiabatic absorber C-401. Two PVDF-packed beds absorb the HCl into weak acid from the water scrubber; the heat of absorption evaporates water so the column runs without a cooler. Regenerated acid leaves the sump at 90 °C and 18.0 % HCl. Water scrubber C-501, fed with 10.1 m³/h of rinse water, and alkaline scrubber C-502 (pH 8.5, NaOH; thiosulphate on ORP) polish the gas to HCl ≤ 10 mg/Nm³ and Cl₂ ≤ 5 mg/Nm³. Induced-draft fans K-501A/B (one standby, VFD) hold the roaster at −0.3 kPa(g) and discharge through 40 m FRP stack ST-501. Fan heat lifts the gas about 14 K above its dew point, so the stack runs dry.

6 Regenerated acid (Area 600)

Acid pumps P-401A/B send the product through graphite cooler E-401 (452 kW, 90 → 45 °C) to tanks T-601A/B. Acid strength is held at 18.0 % by trimming the rinse-water flow (AIC-4001 → FIC-6005). Pumps P-601A/B return acid to the pickling line on demand.

Control philosophy & key loops

How the plant is controlled and which loops matter.

The plant runs from the control room on a redundant Siemens S7-1500 PLC with WinCC SCADA (two operator stations, one engineering station and a historian). Burner management and safety trips run in a separate fail-safe F-CPU rated SIL 2. Field signals use HART; analysers and drives connect over PROFINET.

Three variables decide the plant: roaster top temperature (sets conversion and oxide chloride), roaster draft (keeps HCl inside the plant) and acid strength (sets product quality). Each has a dedicated loop; the rest of the plant follows level and flow.

The operator sets the SPL rate. Feed-forward from roaster feed flow and density moves the gas firing before the temperature drifts; the number of lances in service is chosen automatically so nozzle pressure stays between 5 and 7 bar across 40–110 % load.

Optional packages

Offered separately; each can be added at order or retrofitted.

Option A — Zinc separation unit (ZSU)

For galvanising spent acid with zinc above 0.5 g/L.

Three-column strong-base anion exchange (lead–lag–regen merry-go-round, 3 × 3.2 m³ resin). Zinc is held as the ZnCl₄²⁻ complex and eluted with water as ZnCl₂ solution (≈ 30 g/L Zn) that can return to the flux tank or be sold. Iron passes through to the ARP.

Performance: Feed Zn ≤ 15 g/L → ARP feed Zn ≤ 0.5 g/L; Fe loss ≤ 1 %.

Option B — Heat-recovery condenser & plume abatement

For sites that can use 55–75 °C water (pickling, degreasing and flux bath heating).

Direct-contact condenser column after C-502 with Ti Gr.7 plate exchanger on a closed hot-water loop. Recovers latent heat from the saturated off-gas and returns clean condensate as rinse water.

Performance: Up to 3.5 MW at 70/55 °C; condensate ≈ 5–6 m³/h; visible plume largely eliminated.

Option C — Wet electrostatic precipitator (WESP)

Where the consent order asks for dust/aerosol below 10 mg/Nm³.

Tubular FRP/conductive-FRP WESP between C-502 and the fans, intermittent water wash.

Performance: Dust and HCl aerosol ≤ 5 mg/Nm³.

Option D — Desilication & premium oxide package

To sell ferrite-grade oxide at a premium.

Scrap-iron reactors raise SPL pH to ≈ 3.5, flocculant co-precipitates colloidal silica, filter press removes it before the venturi.

Performance: SiO₂ in oxide ≤ 0.01 %; Fe₂O₃ ≥ 99.3 % (for Mn ≤ 0.4 g/L).

Option E — Dual-fuel back-up (LPG / propane)

Protects regeneration during gas-network outages.

LPG vaporiser skid, mixing/ratio station and dual-fuel burner trains with automatic change-over.

Performance: Full capacity on LPG; change-over without roaster cool-down.

Option F — Two-year operating spares

Lances, nozzle inserts, pump wet-ends and seals, lined-valve kits, filter elements, bag-filter bags and a refractory repair kit, sized for two years of operation.

Options A and B are shown as amber ghosts in the 3D model with space reserved on SH-ARP-GA-01.
