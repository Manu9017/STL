    01Mechanism & forces

    Two nesting quadrant shells hang on one Ø70 shaft. The drawbar pushes a crosshead down the hanger slots; four links drive lugs on the shell end plates (R330, −10° when closed). Push-to-close puts the full piston bore on the closing stroke. Lips meet 51 mm before the cylinder bottoms, so the full force clamps the dross.

    Jaw θCrosshead*Rod travelLink ∠Lip spanF @5 barF @6 bar

    *Crosshead pin centre above main shaft centre. Rod travel from fully retracted (jaws open). Forces at the lip (R350), per shell, cylinder efficiency 0.85, pin friction neglected.

    Why this geometry

    • Lugs sit on the end plates, outside the shell skins, and links run beyond the skin ends (inner links z ±336, outer z ±352). Nothing moving passes through the bowl, so dross never fouls the linkage.
    • Link angle stays 20°–36° off vertical across the stroke: no toggle, no dead point, nearly flat force curve (3.5–4.7 kN at 5 bar).
    • Crosshead runs in 44 mm slots in the two hanger plates: guided and anti-rotation without bushes. Drawbar runs in the 70 mm gap between mast channel webs.
    • All submerged pins have 2 mm diametral clearance. Zinc wets steel and freezes in tight fits; clearance and cycling the jaws while hot keep them free.
    • Inner shell (618 long) nests 3 mm inside the outer shell end plates (656 long), so the closed bowl is sealed at the ends.

    05Pneumatic circuit

    Circuit logic

    • Lever to CLOSE pressurises A (cap end): cylinder extends, crosshead goes down, jaws close. Release lever: spring centre vents A and B, pilot-check valves lock both ports, jaws hold.
    • Exhaust centre is required: a closed-centre valve would trap pilot pressure and the check valves would not seat.
    • Speed is set at the operator stand with the exhaust flow controls (target 4 s per stroke). Nothing needs adjusting on the hot grab.
    • Air consumption: cylinder 17.5 L per double stroke = 121 NL at 6 bar; hoses add 26 NL. Plant compressor demand during dross removal ≈ 150 NL per bite, about 0.1 m³/min averaged.
    • Test after every reconnection: lever CLOSE must close the jaws. Keyed couplings prevent swapped lines, which would reverse the lever.

    06Operating procedure

    1. Plan the session. Remove dross after at least 6 h without dipping (overnight settling), bath at normal working temperature. Do not stir the bath before grabbing.
    2. Pre-start checks. 6 bar at FRL; couplings connected; lever CLOSE closes the jaws; pins move freely; lip wear bars intact; hose sleeves in place; rim rest pinned at the set hole on both sides.
    3. Dry the grab. Hang with the shells 200–300 mm above zinc for 10 min. Repeat after any washing, rain or condensation. A wet grab causes a zinc eruption.
    4. Skim and lower. Skim surface ash at the bite position. Open jaws in air, lower at creep speed until the rim rest lands on both kettle flanges. Slack the hook about 50 mm.
    5. Bite. Hold lever at CLOSE until lips meet (≈ 4 s), hold 3 s more, release to centre.
    6. Lift and drain. Take up slack, lift at creep for the first 500 mm. Stop with the bowl 100–200 mm above zinc for 30 s so free zinc drains back through the perforations.
    7. Dump. Traverse at creep to the pre-heated, dry dross moulds. Lower to 150 mm above the mould, OPEN, cycle the jaws twice to shake out.
    8. Next bite. Step 500–600 mm along the kettle per bite, working from one end. Keep cycling the jaws in air between bites while hot, so zinc does not freeze at the pins.
    9. Park. Close jaws, park on the stand over a drip tray, isolate and vent air, disconnect couplings and cap them, return hoses to the reel.
    Exclusion zone 3 m around the kettle while the grab moves. Operator at the valve stand with face shield, aluminised jacket and gloves. Nobody under the suspended grab. Never let the grab strike kettle walls or floor: kettle damage is the costliest failure in the plant. Do not use the grab to lift anything else.
