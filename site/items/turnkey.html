Overview

A galvanizing plant earns its money in the zinc kettle and loses it everywhere else: in iron carried into the flux, in wet work that spits zinc, in a furnace that overheats one wall, in acid that is dumped too early. SHIVAS designs the whole plant as one process so those losses are engineered out before steel is cut.

We start from your article list and tonnage, fix the kettle size and the dip cycle, and build everything else around that cycle: the pretreatment line, the acid room, flux regeneration, dryer, furnace and burners, fume extraction, cranes, quench and passivation, controls, and the effluent and acid systems from SHIVAS Reinplast. You get one layout, one P&ID, one commissioning team and one point of contact.

Why it pays

Sized on your steel

Kettle, crane and line are sized from your real articles and monthly tonnage, not from a catalogue size.

Lower zinc per tonne

Flux regeneration, a proper dryer and even furnace heating reduce dross, ash and over-coating, the biggest controllable cost in the plant.

Fume and acid under control

Acid enclosed room, lip extraction, scrubber and zinc fume filter are part of the design, not an afterthought for the pollution board.

Fabricated in our shops

PP-H, FRP and mild-steel tanks, ducting, scrubbers and structures are made in-house; bought-out items are specified and integrated by us.

Commissioned and trained

We dry out the furnace, fill and heat the kettle, run the first loads with your team and hand over operating limits that protect the kettle.

How it works

Batch HDG plant

Watch it run →

A complete batch plant in plan. Jigs are loaded, pretreated inside the enclosed acid room, dried, dipped in the kettle under the zinc enclosure, quenched and passivated, then unloaded for dispatch. Fume from the acid room goes to the scrubber, kettle fume to the bag filter, flux runs through regeneration.

Main parts

  1. 1
    Jigging baySteel hung on jigs and loaded.
  2. 2
    DegreasingHot alkaline degrease.
  3. 3
    PicklingFour HCl tanks remove scale and rust.
  4. 4
    RinsingTwo rinses keep acid out of the flux.
  5. 5
    Fluxing + regenerationFlux kept clean by oxidation and filter press.
  6. 6
    Acid room + scrubberEnclosed room, extraction to a packed-bed scrubber.
  7. 7
    Dryer · 4 positionsRecirculating hot air dries the work before the dip.
  8. 8
    Kettle + zinc enclosureDip at about 450 °C under the enclosure.
  9. 9
    ZFE bag filterKettle fume captured and filtered.
  10. 10
    Quench & passivationCools the work and protects the fresh coating.
  11. 11
    Unloading & dispatchInspection, de-jigging and dispatch.
  12. 12
    Overhead cranesCranes carry jigs station to station.

Questions buyers ask

How long does a new plant take?

It depends on size and civil work. Kettle and furnace are usually the longest items; we give a programme with the offer once the layout is fixed.

Can you upgrade an existing plant instead?

Yes. Many projects start with the furnace, flux regeneration or fume control, which pay back quickest.

Do you supply only equipment, or erect it too?

Both. We can supply, erect and commission, or supervise your contractor.
