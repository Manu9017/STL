Overview

Every load carries a little iron from the pickling acid into the flux. Iron in the flux goes on into the zinc and forms dross, a zinc-iron alloy that sinks and has to be removed and sold cheaply. Flux with high iron also gives poorer coatings.

A flux regeneration unit pumps flux through a reactor where iron is oxidised and precipitated, then through a filter press that removes the iron sludge. Clean flux returns to the tank. pH, density and temperature are kept in range, so the flux works properly and lasts.

How it works

Flux regeneration · filter press

Watch it run →

Iron dragged in from pickling builds up in the flux. The regeneration loop oxidises it and raises pH so it drops out as iron hydroxide, and a filter press removes it as cake. Clean flux goes back to the tank.

Main parts

  1. 1
    Flux tankFlux carrying dissolved iron from pickling.
  2. 2
    Transfer pumpDraws flux to the reactor.
  3. 3
    DosingHydrogen peroxide oxidises the iron; ammonia adjusts pH.
  4. 4
    ReactorAgitated vessel where iron hydroxide forms.
  5. 5
    Filter pressPlates trap iron hydroxide as cake.
  6. 6
    Cake dischargePlates open one by one and the cake drops out.
  7. 7
    Clean flux returnFiltrate goes back to the flux tank.
