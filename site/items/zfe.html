Zinc fume extraction

Overview

Kettle fume is a fine, sticky mix of zinc chloride, ammonium chloride and zinc oxide particles. It must be captured at the kettle and filtered before discharge to meet emission limits and keep the shed clear.

SHIVAS zinc fume systems combine a kettle enclosure or side hood, ducting, a bag filter house with pulse-jet cleaning, a fan and a stack. Bag material, air-to-cloth ratio and hopper heating are chosen for chloride fume, which can absorb moisture and blind the bags if the system is designed for ordinary dust.

How it works

Zinc fume extraction

Watch it run →

White fume from the kettle is held by the zinc enclosure, drawn through a bag filter and released clean. Pulse jets knock dust off one row of bags at a time, so filtering never stops.

Main parts

  1. 1
    Zinc enclosureContains fume while work is dipped.
  2. 2
    Fume ductCarries fume to the filter.
  3. 3
    Filter bagsDust collects on the outside of the bags.
  4. 4
    Pulse-jet headerShort bursts of air clean each row in turn.
  5. 5
    Hopper + rotary valveDust leaves without letting air in.
  6. 6
    ID fanKeeps the system under suction.
  7. 7
    ChimneyClean exhaust.

Questions buyers ask

Why do bags blind?

Chloride fume absorbs moisture. Correct air-to-cloth ratio, insulation and cleaning keep the cake dry and porous.
