Overview

The kettle is the most expensive single item in a galvanizing plant and the one that stops everything when it fails. Molten zinc slowly dissolves the steel wall; the rate rises sharply above about 480 °C on the inner face, so a kettle's life is decided by its steel, its welds and how evenly the furnace heats it.

SHIVAS kettles are fabricated from low-silicon, low-carbon kettle-grade plate, with corners formed on our press so the welds sit in the straight walls. Top flange, stiffeners and wall thickness are designed for the zinc head and the furnace layout. We size length, width and depth from your largest article, the dip cycle and the zinc mass needed to hold temperature through a load.

Why it pays

Longer life

Low-silicon steel, formed corners and controlled welding slow zinc attack at the weak points.

Right size

Enough zinc to hold temperature under your heaviest load, without paying to keep spare zinc molten.

Monitored walls

Wall thermocouple pockets let the furnace control the steel temperature, not just the zinc.

Replacements

We build to the size of your existing furnace so a replacement drops in with minimum downtime.

Questions buyers ask

How long does a kettle last?

Life depends mostly on wall temperature, heating evenness and steel chemistry of the work. A well-run kettle commonly lasts several years; local overheating can halve that.

Why not just make the kettle bigger?

Every extra tonne of zinc has to be bought and kept molten. We size the zinc mass to about ten times the hourly throughput so temperature holds without waste.

Can you check our old kettle?

Yes. Wall thickness readings and a look at the furnace help decide whether to replace now or plan it.
