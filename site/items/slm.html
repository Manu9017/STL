Overview

In a straight line machine each block pulls the wire through its die and passes it straight to the next die, without the wire looping over the top. Every block has its own motor and drive, and the drives keep the blocks in step as the wire elongates through each die, so the wire is never twisted or slack.

SHIVAS straight line machines are built for steady, high-output drawing: rigid fabricated frames, hardened capstans, water-cooled blocks and die boxes, and a control system that ramps all blocks together. The number of blocks follows from rod size, finished size and the reduction per pass we design with you.

How it works

Straight line wire drawing machine

Watch it run →

Wire runs from block to block in a straight line, with no twist. The blocks are inclined: the wire leaves the top of each block over a dancer roller and goes straight down into the die box of the next block. The dancer senses wire tension and the PLC trims each block’s AC drive to hold it steady. At every die the wire gets thinner and faster.

Main parts

  1. 1
    PayoffRod coil feeds the line.
  2. 2
    Die boxRotating die box with drawing soap before every block; the die sizes the wire.
  3. 3
    Inclined drawing blockWater-cooled capstan set at an angle; the wire enters at the foot, wraps a few turns and leaves at the top.
  4. 4
    Dancer armRoller on a sprung arm between blocks; it rises and falls with tension and tells the PLC to trim block speed.
  5. 5
    Motor and gearboxAC inverter drive for each block.
  6. 6
    Finished wireOut to the dead block coiler or spooler.

Questions buyers ask

Straight line or OTO?

Straight line for high-carbon and quality wire with no twist; OTO for flexibility and lower cost on low-carbon wire. Our blog article compares them.

How many blocks?

Total reduction divided by a sensible reduction per pass; we design the die sequence with you.
