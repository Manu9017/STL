Flux heating system

Flux kept warm with heat recovered from the quench tank, so work leaves the flux warm and dries fast, and the quench water stays cool.

Overview

Warm flux wets the steel better and the work dries faster, which means less moisture reaches the zinc. Work comes out of the kettle at about 450 °C and dumps most of that heat into the quench tank, which then has to be cooled. SHIVAS uses that heat for the flux instead of burning fuel for it.

Hot quench water is pumped through a plate heat exchanger or an in-tank PP / PVDF coil in the flux tank and back to the quench. A three-way valve under PID control keeps the flux at its set point, and the quench water is cooled at the same time.

Questions buyers ask

Is there enough heat in the quench?

Galvanized work enters the quench at about 450 °C, so every tonne dipped brings heat in. On normal production that is more than the flux tank loses; a small top-up heater covers start-up after long stops.

Does it change the quench?

It helps: the quench water runs cooler, which is what you want for quenching and passivation.
