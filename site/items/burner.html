Overview

A high-velocity burner completes combustion in its own refractory chamber and leaves the nozzle as a fast jet of hot gas. The jet entrains furnace gas and keeps it moving round the kettle, so heat reaches the wall by convection over a large area instead of radiating from a flame onto one patch of steel.

SHIVAS engineers the combustion system around the kettle: number and position of burners, firing pattern, air and gas trains, ignition and flame supervision, and the control logic that pulses the burners between high and low fire to hold temperature.

How it works

High-velocity burner

Watch it run →

Inside one burner: gas through the central lance, combustion air through the swirler, a refractory chamber where the flame forms and a nozzle that throws hot gas out at high velocity. The burner control unit proves air, purges, then lights the main gas directly with a spark. No pilot.

Main parts

  1. 1
    Gas lanceCarries fuel gas to the burner head.
  2. 2

  3. 3
    SwirlerSpins the air so it mixes fast with the gas.
  4. 4

  6. 6

  7. 7

  9. 9
    Burner control unitRuns every start in the same order; locks out on any fault.
  10. 10

  11. 11
    Ratio controllerMeters gas to match the air pressure across the range.

Questions buyers ask

Why high velocity rather than flat flame?

The fast jet circulates furnace gas so the whole wall is heated; flat flames tend to heat the wall nearest the burner.

Can you retrofit our furnace?

Often, yes. We check casing, lining and burner positions first.
