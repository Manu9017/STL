Overview

Threaded parts cannot be hung and drained like structural steel; excess zinc would fill the threads. In spin galvanizing the parts are loaded into perforated baskets, dipped, lifted and spun at speed inside a fixed spin hood just above the bath, so excess zinc is thrown off and runs back into the kettle.

SHIVAS designs spin plants around your fastener mix and tonnage: pretreatment for small parts, basket handling, the kettle and furnace, the spinner, quench, passivation and weighing.

The process, step by step

  1. 1
    Weigh and fill

    Parts are weighed into perforated baskets: one part number per basket, each with its own recipe.

  2. 2
    Pretreatment

    Degreasing, pickling, rinsing and fluxing in rotating drums in PP-H tanks inside an enclosed acid room.

  3. 3
    Dry

    A flue-gas-heated dryer brings the parts up to temperature; a dry check stops any wet basket going into the zinc.

  4. 4
    Dip

    The dip-spin head lowers the basket into the zinc and agitates it so gas escapes from nuts and recesses.

  5. 5
    Clear the surface

    Two red vanes on the basket and a red skimming flap push the ash over the weir, so the basket comes up through a clean surface.

  6. 6
    Spin

    The basket rises into the fixed spin hood and spins; flung zinc runs back into the kettle while still liquid.

  7. 7
    Discharge

    The basket opens at one station and the parts drop into the quench.

  8. 8
    Quench and passivate

    The parts drop through a perforated plate into the water quench, then a separate Cr(III) passivation limits white rust.

  9. 9
    Dry, inspect, weigh

    Air knife and belt dryer, an inspection belt, and output weighing for every lot.

Questions buyers ask

Can a structural plant do fasteners?

Only with a centrifuge; dipping alone fills threads. A spin line beside the main kettle is common.
