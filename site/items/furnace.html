Overview

A furnace has two jobs that pull against each other: put enough heat into the zinc to recover from each dip, and never let any part of the kettle wall get too hot. Hot spots dissolve the kettle and make dross. SHIVAS furnaces use high-velocity burners that drive hot gas along the walls, or electric elements in zones, so the heat flux is spread over the whole wall.

The casing is lined with ceramic fibre modules for low heat storage, the combustion space is shaped so the gas circulates round the kettle, and the flue gas is taken off to the dryer instead of the stack. Temperatures in the zinc and on the wall are measured and controlled by PLC.

Fast recovery

Burner turndown and pulse firing recover the bath after heavy dips.

Natural gas, LPG, LDO or furnace oil, or a fully electric furnace.

How it works

Furnace · 2 HV burners

Watch it run →

Two high-velocity burners fire from the two end faces of the furnace, diagonally opposite, each one along a long wall. Pulse firing switches each burner between high and low fire on a timed cycle, so the jets keep the hot gas circling the kettle and the wall heats evenly at any load.

Shown running as: Pulse firing · Steady high fire

Questions buyers ask

How much fuel will it use?

Fuel per tonne depends on throughput and idle hours, because the bath loses heat all day. The plant calculator shows the split.

Can we change from oil to gas later?

Yes, with a dual-fuel burner and gas train planned at the start.
