Hot-dip galvanizing plants

Galvanizing protects steel from rust. The steel is cleaned, then dipped in molten zinc at about 450 °C, and the zinc bonds to it as a hard coat that lasts for decades. We design, build and start up complete galvanizing plants, or supply any part of one.

  • Complete plants, or single items for a running plant
  • Zinc kettles, furnaces and burners
  • Cleaning tanks, fume extraction and controls

Wire drawing machines

Wire drawing makes wire from steel rod. The rod is pulled through a row of dies, each a little smaller than the one before, until it is the size you need. We build the machines that do it, and complete lines from rod coil to finished wire.

  • Straight line and OTO drawing machines
  • Payoffs, pointing, welding, coilers and spoolers
  • Complete wire lines, including galvanizing

    01 / 0950 °CAlkaline degreaser · 40–60 °C

    Degreasing

    Oil, grease and drawing soap come off first. Zinc only bonds to clean steel, so this bath sets the result for everything after it.

    ‹ Swipe the line ›

    Swipe or drag the line sideways to move the beam from tank to tank, or tap a step. Left alone, it runs by itself.

    The heart of the plant

    Zinc kettles & furnaces

    The kettle carries hundreds of tonnes of zinc at 450 °C and is heated through its own walls, every hour of the year. SHIVAS designs kettle, furnace and burner layout together, because how heat reaches the wall decides how long a kettle lives.

    Kettle size check. Drag the sliders or pick an example size. The 3D drawing is to scale, with a 1.75 m person for reference: length, width and depth all change as you drag. It shows the kettle with its flange and stiffeners, the molten zinc and, dashed in amber, the largest article it takes.

    Zinc in bath352t
    Bath surface14.4m²
    Largest article it takes (L × B × H)8.4 × 1.2 × 3.3 m

    How we size a kettle: inside length = article length + 100 mm; inside width = article width + 100 mm; depth = article height + 100 mm of zinc below the article + 100 mm left empty at the top. The zinc figure fills the kettle to 100 mm below the top.

    Zinc at 6.6 t/m³ with the bath 100 mm below the flange. Article envelope is indicative; final clearances are set in design. Work out your zinc consumption →

    Kettle

    • Corners bent on our press, seams in the straight walls
    • Kettle-grade low-silicon, low-carbon steel plate
    • Plate and stiffening sized to zinc head and wall temperature
    • Sized to your largest article: + 100 mm in length and width, + 200 mm in depth
    • Wall thermocouples for temperature profile logging
    • Replacement kettles for running plants

    Furnace

    • Two high-velocity burners at opposite corners
    • Dual-fuel system with full gas and oil trains
    • Even heat flux, no hot spots eating into the plate
    • Ceramic fibre lining for fast heat-up and low losses
    • Natural gas, LPG or oil firing with PID control
    • Flue gas recovered to the dryer; quench heat recovered to warm the flux

    Around the bath

    • Zinc enclosure feeding the fume bag filter
    • Dross grab and zinc pump
    • Zinc ash recovery on site
    • Burner management and SCADA logging
    • Kettle change and furnace relining service

    What leaves the plant, and how we treat it

    A pickling, galvanizing or wire line gives off acid fume, waste water and spent acid. Each is handled by a complete system that SHIVAS Reinplast Company of India designs, builds and commissions.

    Air

    Acid fume

    From the pickling tanks and the acid enclosed room

    Acid fume extraction (AFE)

    Suction hoods→FRP ducting→Packed-bed scrubber→FRP blower→Chimney

    AFE is the complete system: hoods, ducting, scrubber, blower and chimney. Clean air leaves the chimney; the scrubber liquor goes to the effluent plant.

    Equalisation→Neutralisation→Clarifier→Filter press→Sand and carbon filters

    Fumeless, explained

    What keeps the acid in the tank

    1. 1FRP lidClose-fitting covers over every acid and hot tank, in sections that lift for maintenance.
    2. 2Water sealThe lid skirt sits in a water trough, so there's no gap for fume to escape.
    3. 3Slot extractionSlotted ducts along the wall draw off vapour at the surface and keep the space under the lid at slight suction.
    4. 4Wire guidesCeramic or PP guides at small entry and exit slots; the strands pass through and air only flows in.
    5. 5To the scrubberFRP duct to a packed-bed scrubber and FRP blower, then the chimney.
    6. 6Acid at ambientHCl with inhibitor, held at low temperature with recirculation and filtration, so less fume forms to begin with.
