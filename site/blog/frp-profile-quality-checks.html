Tanks and FRP

Checking pultruded FRP profiles: cure, glass content and dimensions

A pultruded profile can look right and still be under-cured or short of glass. Checks for a delivery: surface, Barcol hardness, burn-off, size and stiffness.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 2 min read

Pultruded FRP profiles carry walkways, gratings, cable trays and supports in places where steel would rust away. A profile can look perfect and still be under-cured, short of glass or out of tolerance, and the first sign is a walkway that sags or a joint that cracks. A few checks when a delivery arrives catch most of the problems.

1. Look at the surface

  • A smooth, even, resin-rich surface, with the surface veil intact.
  • No bare or loose fibres, no white dry patches where the resin has not wetted the glass, no cracks, blisters or chipped edges.
  • Even colour along the length, a sign of steady pigment and cure.
  • Cut ends free of voids and separating layers.

2. Check the cure: Barcol hardness

A Barcol impressor, used as ASTM D2583 describes, presses a point into the surface and reads its hardness, and an under-cured profile reads low. Take several readings on resin-rich surface along each profile, and compare their average with the value the resin system should reach. A quick extra check: a surface that turns tacky under a cloth wetted with acetone is under-cured.

3. Check the glass: burn-off

The glass gives the strength and the stiffness. A small weighed sample is heated in a furnace until the resin burns away and the residue is weighed: the loss on ignition gives the resin content, and the residue the glass and any mineral filler, as ASTM D2584 and ISO 1172 describe. Compare it with the supplier's data. Low glass means low stiffness.

4. Check the dimensions

Measure the height, width, wall and flange thickness, the squareness of the flanges, straightness and twist against the drawing and its tolerances. EN 13706 sets tolerances for pultruded profiles where the order calls for it. Thin walls and twisted sections cause trouble at every joint.

5. Check the stiffness

FRP structures are usually sized for deflection, so the modulus is the property the design leans on. EN 13706 defines two grades, E23 and E17, with a lengthwise tensile modulus of at least 23 and 17 GPa. Coupons cut from a profile can be tested in tension (ISO 527-4) and in bending (ISO 14125), and a bending test on a full length of profile gives its effective modulus. Ask for the batch certificate, and test when the use is critical.

Storage and handling

  • Store profiles flat and supported along their length; a long profile left on two points takes a set.
  • Keep them out of long exposure to sun if they will wait months before erection.
  • Seal every cut end and drilled hole with resin on site. See designing with FRP profiles.
