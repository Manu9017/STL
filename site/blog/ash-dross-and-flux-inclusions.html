Coating defects

Ash, dross and flux inclusions

Lumps in the coating look alike but come from three different places: the top of the bath, the bottom of the kettle, and the flux film. How to tell them apart and stop them.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited4 November 2026 · 2 min read

Ash inclusions

Zinc ash is the oxide and chloride crust that forms on the bath surface, mostly from the flux burning off as work goes in. If the work comes out through that crust, it carries ash with it. Ash inclusions are dull, grey and powdery, and they sit on the surface, usually on the top side of the work and in lines where it left the bath.

  • Skim the bath surface clear at the withdrawal point just before the work comes out.
  • Use the right flux strength and dry the work well: wet or over-fluxed work makes far more ash.
  • Keep the bath temperature steady; a cold bath holds ash.
  • Recover the ash: it is mostly zinc and worth money.

Dross pimples

Dross is a zinc–iron alloy that forms in the bath from iron carried in on the work and dissolved from the kettle wall. It is heavier than zinc and settles on the kettle floor. When it is stirred up it sticks to the work as small, hard pimples that feel rough to the hand.

  • Do not dip the work too close to the kettle floor, and keep the dross layer low by regular drossing.
  • Avoid violent movements and dipping too fast, which stir the floor.
  • Keep iron out of the bath: good rinsing after pickling and a flux bath kept low in iron with flux regeneration.
  • Hold the bath temperature steady; swings bring dross up.

Flux inclusions

Flux salts that do not burn off cleanly in the zinc stay trapped under or in the coating as black or brown spots. They hold moisture and can start corrosion later. The usual causes are flux that is too strong or too thick, work that goes into the zinc wet, and flux that has pooled in pockets.

  • Hold flux strength in range and let the work drain over the flux tank.
  • Dry the work fully before dipping.
  • Dip at a steady, not too slow, speed so the flux burns off evenly.
  • Hang the work so flux cannot pool in pockets.
