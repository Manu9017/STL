Lime or caustic soda: how the neutraliser shapes your ZLD plant

Lime costs less; caustic soda gives a salt that crystallizes. How the neutraliser decides the sludge, the salt, the evaporator and the dry product of ZLD.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 2 min read

Choosing a neutraliser for acid effluent looks like a simple purchase: the cheapest alkali that brings the pH up. In a plant that ends in zero liquid discharge, the choice reaches all the way to the dry salt at the end of the chain. The neutraliser decides what that salt is, how it behaves in the evaporator, and what you are left holding.

The chemistry

Pickling effluent carries hydrochloric acid and iron chloride. Neutralising it swaps the hydrogen and the iron for the metal of the alkali, and the chloride stays in the water:

  • Lime: 2HCl + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O, and FeCl₂ + Ca(OH)₂ → Fe(OH)₂ + CaCl₂. The salt is calcium chloride.
  • Caustic soda: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O, and FeCl₂ + 2NaOH → Fe(OH)₂ + 2NaCl. The salt is sodium chloride.

Either way, aeration turns the iron into ferric hydroxide, which settles and is pressed into cake. For each kilogram of HCl, lime needs about 1.02 kg of Ca(OH)₂ and caustic about 1.10 kg of NaOH; for each kilogram of iron, about 1.33 kg of lime or 1.43 kg of caustic.

What each one does downstream

AspectHydrated limeCaustic sodaLime, then soda ash
Reagent costLowestHigherBetween, two reagents
SludgeIron hydroxide and lime residueIron hydroxide only, the leastIron hydroxide and calcium carbonate, the most
Salt in the waterCalcium chlorideSodium chlorideSodium chloride
In the evaporatorBoiling point rises steeply as it concentrates; corrosive when hotModerateModerate

In the third route, soda ash added after the lime precipitates the calcium as calcium carbonate and leaves sodium chloride in the water. It keeps the cheap neutraliser and gives a salt the evaporator and crystallizer handle well, at the price of more sludge.

The example worked three ways

On the 90 m³ a day example in our ZLD design article, with a 4-effect MEE, a crystallizer and an ATFD, the calculator gives:

With lime, almost all the salt ends up in the ATFD as calcium chloride. With either sodium route, most of it leaves the crystallizer as sodium chloride crystals and the ATFD dries only a small purge.

How to choose

  • If the dry salt must be easy to handle, or may be reused, a sodium route makes sense.
  • If sludge disposal is the bigger cost, caustic soda gives the least cake.
  • If the reagent bill dominates and the plant can handle a product that draws moisture, lime is cheapest.
  • In every case the biggest saving is upstream: less spent acid in the ETP means less of every salt. See what to do with spent pickling acid.
