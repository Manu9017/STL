Coating quality

Repairing galvanized steel: what ISO 1461 and ASTM A780 allow

Bare spots, weld burns and handling damage can be repaired, within limits. Sizes, repair methods and thickness, and when re-galvanizing is the only answer.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 3 min read

Two kinds of damage need repair on galvanized steel. The first is an area the kettle missed: a bare spot found at inspection, before the work leaves the galvanizer. The second is damage done afterwards, by welding, cutting, drilling or rough handling on site. ISO 1461 sets the limits for the first. ASTM A780 describes the repair methods for both.

What ISO 1461 lets the galvanizer repair

  • No single uncoated area may be larger than 10 cm², about a 3 × 3 cm square.
  • All the uncoated areas together may not exceed 0.5 per cent of the article's surface.
  • Beyond either limit the article is re-galvanized, unless the purchaser agrees otherwise.

On a beam with 30 m² of surface, 0.5 per cent is 0.15 m², or 1,500 cm², so the total is rarely the problem; the 10 cm² limit on a single area is. The required thickness of the repair has changed between editions. The 2009 edition asked for the repair to be at least 30 µm thicker than the local coating required. The 2022 edition asks for at least 100 µm on the repaired area unless the parties agree otherwise, for example when the steel will be painted. Check which edition the order names.

The three repair methods

MethodHow it is doneBest forWatch for
Zinc-rich paintBrushed or sprayed in several coats onto cleaned steelSmall areas, cut ends, site workBuilds slowly; measure the dry film; needs a clean, dry surface
Zinc thermal sprayMolten zinc sprayed by flame or arc onto blast-cleaned steelLarger areas; the closest match to galvanizingNeeds blasting and equipment; edges must overlap sound zinc
Zinc-based solderThe area is heated to about 315 °C and a zinc alloy stick is rubbed onSmall spots and edges in the shopKeep below about 400 °C or the coating around it burns; needs a practised hand

ASTM A780 sets the zinc content of the dry paint film for zinc-rich paints: either 65 to 69 per cent, or above 92 per cent, metallic zinc by weight. A cheap aerosol sold as galvanizing spray, with little zinc in it, looks the part and protects far less.

Preparing the area

  • Remove loose zinc, rust, slag and spatter with a wire brush, grinder or blasting, back to bright steel.
  • Carry the cleaning a little way into the sound coating around it, so the repair overlaps good zinc.
  • Keep the area dry and free of oil. Paint over damp steel or over salts lifts within months.
  • Measure the finished repair: dry film thickness for paint, gauge readings for spray.

Welding and cutting on site

Welding galvanized steel burns the zinc off beside the weld and gives off zinc oxide fume. Grind the zinc back from the joint before welding, use fume extraction or respiratory protection, then repair the burnt zone with zinc-rich paint or zinc spray. Small cut ends and drilled holes are partly protected by the zinc around them, but the edges of holes and cut ends on outdoor work last much longer when they are painted with a zinc-rich paint the same day.

When to re-galvanize instead

  • A single bare area over 10 cm², or bare areas over 0.5 per cent of the surface.
  • Bare spots that keep coming back in the same place: trapped air from a missing vent hole, or a surface the pretreatment cannot clean. Re-dipping the same design gives the same result, so fix the cause first.
  • Large areas of flaking or peeling coating.

Re-galvanizing means stripping the zinc in acid and sending the article through the line again. Strip in a separate tank, not in the pickling acid: zinc in the pickling acid causes trouble in the kettle and in acid recovery.

Before you accept a repair

  • Each repaired area under 10 cm², and all of them under 0.5 per cent of the surface.
  • The method recorded, and one the order allows.
  • The surface cleaned to bright steel, and dry.
  • The repair overlapping sound zinc all round.
  • The repair thickness measured and meeting the edition of ISO 1461 on the order.
