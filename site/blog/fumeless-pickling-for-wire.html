Fumeless pickling for wire: keeping the acid inside the tank

Continuous wire lines can pickle without filling the shed with acid fume. How covered, controlled tanks change the economics.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited16 July 2026 · 2 min read

Open hydrochloric acid tanks on a wire galvanizing line release fume that corrodes everything nearby: rolls, take-ups, the building and the electrics. Fumeless pickling keeps the acid inside covered tanks so very little fume escapes, and what does is drawn off to a small scrubber.

How it works

  • Tanks are closed with lids, and the strands pass through narrow slots.
  • Acid strength, temperature and iron are held in a narrow band, so the acid works efficiently without heating that drives off fume.
  • A small extraction keeps the tank space under slight negative pressure.
  • Rinsing is cascaded to cut water use and carry-over.

What it saves

  • The building and equipment are not exposed to acid fume.
  • Extraction and scrubber are much smaller than for open tanks.
  • Acid use falls because less is lost and the bath is controlled.
  • The line is a better place to work.

Upstream of galvanizing or drawing

Fumeless pickling suits continuous lines: ahead of hot-dip or electro-galvanizing, and in some plants ahead of drawing. Combined with acid recovery, the spent acid becomes a smaller and more manageable stream.
