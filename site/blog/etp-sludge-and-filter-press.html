ETP sludge: settling, filter press sizing and cake handling

Neutralising pickling effluent makes hydroxide sludge every day. How much, how to settle and press it, how big the press must be and how to dry the cake.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 3 min read

Every kilogram of iron in pickling effluent leaves the ETP as nearly 2 kg of dry ferric hydroxide and, with the water still in it, several kilograms of filter cake. Sludge is a daily cost in disposal, handling and space, and the oxidation tank, the clarifier and the filter press decide how much of it there is and how wet it is.

How much sludge

  • Ferric hydroxide: 1.91 kg of dry solids for each kg of iron.
  • Zinc hydroxide, where zinc is present: 1.52 kg for each kg of zinc.
  • Lime residue: the excess lime and the impurities in it, which add to the solids.
  • Calcium carbonate, if soda ash follows the lime: about 0.94 kg for each kg of soda ash.

Divide the dry solids by the dryness of the cake to get the cake. At 35 per cent solids, a kilogram of dry solids becomes nearly 3 kg of cake.

Settle it well first

  • Oxidise fully. Ferrous hydroxide is fine and slimy; ferric hydroxide settles and filters far better. Enough air and enough time in the oxidation tank pay back in the clarifier and in the press.
  • Hold the pH. Iron and zinc come out best at about pH 8.5 to 9.5. A drifting pH leaves metals in the water and makes weak flocs.
  • Flocculate. A polymer dose of a few milligrams per litre gathers the fine particles into flocs that settle.
  • Size the clarifier on surface area. The settling rate decides how many cubic metres an hour each square metre can take. For metal hydroxides about 0.5 to 1.5 m³ per m² an hour is usual, and 1 is a sensible starting point.

Sizing the filter press

A filter press is sized on the volume of cake it must hold in one cycle. Hydroxide cake weighs roughly 1.2 to 1.35 t per m³. Divide the cake made in a day by its density and by the number of press cycles a day. In the 90 m³ a day example in our ZLD design article, lime neutralisation makes about 2.2 t of cake a day, about 1.7 m³. Pressed twice a day, the press needs about 850 litres of chamber volume. Caustic soda would make about 1.8 t, and lime followed by soda ash about 4.7 t.

Drier cake, less to haul

  • A membrane filter press squeezes the cake after filling and gives drier cake than a plain recessed plate press.
  • Let each cycle finish. Cutting the filling short to save time leaves wet cake.
  • Keep the cloths clean. Blinded cloths filter slowly and leave wet patches.
  • Feed the press from a thickened sludge, not straight from a dilute underflow.

Handling and disposal

Cake from pickling and galvanizing effluent carries iron and zinc compounds and is disposed of as the plant's consent conditions require, usually through an authorised facility. Store it under cover on a sealed floor that drains back to the ETP, and keep a record of every load that leaves.
