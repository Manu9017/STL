What to do with spent pickling acid: regenerate, recover or neutralise

Spent acid is a small stream carrying most of the iron and salt. Regeneration, acid recovery, sale and neutralisation compared, with their effect on the ETP.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 2 min read

Spent pickling acid is a small stream with a large effect. A plant pickling 1,500 t of steel a month may produce only 60 to 70 m³ of it, yet that acid carries most of the iron and chloride that leave the pickling line. What happens to it decides how much lime the ETP uses, how much sludge it makes, and how much salt a ZLD plant has to dry.

What spent acid is

As hydrochloric acid pickles steel, it turns into iron chloride. A bath is spent when free acid has fallen and iron chloride has risen to the point where fresh acid no longer brings it back, typically with a little free acid left and well over 100 g/L of iron. See pickling right for keeping baths working longer.

The options

OptionWhat happensWhat comes backBest for
Acid regeneration by spray roastingThe acid is sprayed into a hot reactor. Iron chloride turns into iron oxide and HCl gas, which is absorbed as fresh acidRegenerated hydrochloric acid, and iron oxideLarge volumes; plants that want to close the acid loop
Free acid recoveryA resin or membrane separates the free acid from the ironThe free acid, for reuseBaths dumped with a lot of free acid still in them
Sale to an authorised userThe iron chloride solution is used elsewhere, for example to make water treatment chemicalsNothing, but no treatment costWhere a reliable buyer and the permissions exist
Neutralisation in the ETPLime or caustic sodaNothing: sludge and dissolved saltSmall volumes, or the last resort

What neutralising it costs

Neutralising one cubic metre of spent acid with 130 g/L of iron and 3 per cent free acid takes about 257 kg of hydrated lime, makes about 844 kg of filter cake at 35 per cent solids, and leaves about 315 kg of calcium chloride dissolved in the water. In a ZLD plant, every kilogram of that salt has to be evaporated and dried, so a cubic metre of spent acid costs far more at the end of the chain than at the start.

Zinc in the acid

Galvanizers who strip rejected work in the pickling tanks add zinc to the iron. Acid carrying both is hard to regenerate and hard to sell. A separate stripping tank keeps the pickling acid clean and lets the zinc be recovered on its own.

Choosing

  • Measure first: spent acid a month, the iron and free acid in it, and the zinc if you strip.
  • Above roughly 300 m³ a month, regeneration usually suits better than neutralising.
  • Below that, free acid recovery and keeping baths working longer cut the volume.
  • Whatever is left goes to the ETP, and the ZLD plant must be sized for it. See designing the whole plant.
