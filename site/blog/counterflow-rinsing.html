Effluent

Counterflow rinsing: cleaner work with a fraction of the water

Rinse water is most of the effluent in many pickling plants. The counterflow rinse equation, worked examples for one to three stages, and cutting the drag-out.

KVKrishan VermaCEO, SHIVAS Technology Limited22 November 2026 · 3 min read

Every load lifted out of a process tank carries a film of the solution with it: the drag-out. The rinse that follows has to dilute that film before the work goes into the next bath. In many pickling and galvanizing shops the rinse water is most of the effluent the ETP and ZLD plant must treat. Counterflow rinsing does the same rinsing with a small fraction of the water.

One rinse tank

With fresh water running through a single rinse tank, the tank settles at a strength where the drag-out coming in balances the overflow going out. To dilute the drag-out a thousand times, say from 150 g/L of acid to 0.15 g/L, the water flow must be about a thousand times the drag-out. At 20 litres an hour of drag-out, that is 20 m³ of water an hour.

Counterflow rinsing

In counterflow rinsing, fresh water enters the last rinse tank and overflows backwards, tank by tank, to the first, while the work moves forwards. The work meets cleaner water at every stage. At steady state:

C0 ÷ Cn = 1 + r + r² + … + rn

where C0 is the strength of the process bath, Cn the strength in the last rinse, n the number of rinse stages and r the rinse ratio: the water flow divided by the drag-out. When r is large, the water needed is close to the drag-out multiplied by the dilution raised to the power 1/n.

Rinse stagesWater for a dilution of 100Water for a dilution of 1,000
One1,980 L/h19,980 L/h
Two, counterflow189 L/h622 L/h
Three, counterflow85 L/h193 L/h

The table is for 20 L/h of drag-out. At a dilution of 1,000, a second counterflow stage cuts the water about thirty times, and a third about a hundred times. Every litre saved is a litre the ETP does not neutralise and the ZLD plant does not evaporate.

Cut the drag-out first

  • Hold each load over the tank long enough to drain, and tilt jigs so the liquid runs off one low corner.
  • Hang work so that pockets drain, and make sure the vent and drain holes are there.
  • A warm bath drains better than a cold one.
  • A fine spray over the process tank as the load lifts returns part of the film to where it came from, where the tank's water balance allows it.

Halving the drag-out halves the rinse water, whatever the number of stages.

Use the rinse water twice

The first rinse, the strongest, is a weak copy of the process bath. It can top up heated tanks that lose water by evaporation, such as degreasing, returning chemicals with the water. In pickling, rinse water can be used to dilute fresh acid when a bath is made up. A conductivity probe in the last rinse, opening the fresh water valve only when the rinse needs it, stops water running while the line stands idle.

What it means for the effluent plant

An ETP and ZLD plant is sized on flow as much as on load. Fewer cubic metres mean smaller tanks, a smaller RO and a smaller evaporator, and less steam or power every day. See designing the whole plant.
